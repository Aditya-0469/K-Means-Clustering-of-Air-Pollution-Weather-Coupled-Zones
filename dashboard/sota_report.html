1. Introduction

Air pollution remains one of the most pressing environmental and public health challenges in India, with the Delhi National Capital Region (NCR) consistently ranking among the most polluted urban agglomerations globally [1]. The NCR encompasses a heterogeneous landscape spanning approximately 55,000 km², including Delhi and satellite cities Noida, Ghaziabad, Faridabad, Gurugram, Sonepat, and Bahadurgarh — each exhibiting distinct emission profiles and divergent air quality behaviours.

A critical limitation of the current operational framework is the treatment of Delhi NCR as a spatially homogeneous entity. The Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP), the primary emergency-response mechanism, applies blanket restrictions without differentiating between an industrial corridor in Ghaziabad and a residential green belt in Dwarka [3]. Studies show GRAP's effectiveness remains below 40% [12], with much improvement attributable to meteorological variability rather than policy.

PROJECT OBJECTIVES
O1 — Compile coupled AQ + meteorological feature vectors across 18 Delhi NCR CAAQMS stations (2021–2024)
O2 — Apply K-Means with optimal k selection (Elbow + Silhouette + Davies-Bouldin + Calinski-Harabász)
O3 — Profile each cluster's pollution-weather behaviour signature
O4 — Validate clusters against historical GRAP episode severity records (2021–2025)

2. Literature Review

Research is grouped into four thematic clusters: (A) clustering-based zone identification, (B) meteorological influence, (C) ML forecasting, (D) GRAP validation.

A. CLUSTERING-BASED AIR QUALITY ZONE IDENTIFICATION

Kumar et al. [1] applied hierarchical clustering to 14 Delhi stations (2016–2020), identifying three zones with 87% land-use consistency but no meteorological coupling. Garg & Sharma [2] used K-Means (k=5) with Silhouette=0.61 but only 2 meteorological features. Zhang et al. [3] showed GMM probabilistic clustering for 50 Chinese cities. Rana et al. [4] used DBSCAN to identify 12 PM2.5 hotspots in Noida-Ghaziabad, though its sensitivity to hyperparameters limits reproducibility.

B. METEOROLOGICAL INFLUENCE ON DELHI NCR AIR QUALITY

Sharma et al. [5] demonstrated wind stagnation (WS < 1 m/s) + high humidity amplifies PM2.5 by 2.3–3.8× — directly motivating the stagnation index feature. Venkataraman et al. [6] linked ERA5 BLH < 500m to Severe AQI 78% of the time. Beig et al. [7] showed WRF-CMAQ temperature inversion increases surface PM2.5 by 150–200%.

C. MACHINE LEARNING FORECASTING FOR DELHI NCR

Singh & Rao [8] achieved XGBoost R²=0.87 at Anand Vihar but it is station-specific. Prasad et al. [9] LSTM achieved MAE=18.3 μg/m³ but is unstable during AQI>400 episodes. Patel et al. [11] showed Cluster-Informed RF reduced RMSE by 18% vs. global model in Shanghai — directly motivating this project's zone-based approach.

D. GRAP AND POLLUTION EPISODE VALIDATION

Guttikunda et al. [12] found GRAP Stage III/IV reduces AQI by only 5–12%. Chakraborty et al. [13] found industrial station compliance at 32% vs. residential 61%, confirming need for zone-specific enforcement. Kumar & Nagpure [15] source apportionment: 35–40% biomass, 25% industry — matching Cluster 1's high PM2.5+SO2+NO2 fingerprint.
